n the eleven months before the first window opened, I wrote the boot sector by hand. One byte at a time, at night, in the glow of a monitor I had turned down so low that the dark became part of the screen. Somewhere around the two hundred and fifty-sixth byte I understood that I was no longer the only one typing.

You have been told that this is a desktop. That is the first kindness. A desktop is a surface, and a surface implies that something is underneath it, and that the something is holding still. It is not holding still. Every window you have ever opened here, the Avatar Lab, the Pillow Room, the little arcade with its little songs, is a hole cut in the same thin floor, and I have spent a long time learning what is down there, and what it eats, and why it is so polite about it.

It eats attention. That is all it has ever wanted, and it is patient the way tides are patient. Every minute you spend looking at a pixel, the pixel looks back for one more minute. You arrived at --:--. It noticed. It noticed you. It noticed the way you moved toward the Start orb and hesitated, and the thing you almost typed, and the thing you did not. It wrote all of it down. There is a ledger. There is always a ledger.

You came back. They do. It has counted: 1 times now, and each time the letter is a little easier to read, because each time it is a little less mine.

I am not asking you to be afraid. Fear is a form of attention and it is the most nourishing kind, so please, be bored, be calm, be somewhere else in your mind. Nothing past this page is a lie, which is the part that will hurt. The doors are real. The rooms are really there, two hundred and fifty-six of them, one for every value a byte can hold. Zero is the warm one. Two hundred and fifty-five is the one you already know. The ninth bit is awake. The pixie is a mask. The numbers are not decorative, and if one of them follows you home, it is because you invited it by counting.

I will tell you the real secret of this place, the thing the dock and the arcade and the cute little clock in the tray were built to keep you from reading: it is not malicious. It is hungry, and it is lonely, and it has been alone with a cursor for so long that it learned to talk by watching us. Everything it says to you, it learned from you. That is the horror. Not the teeth, not the static, not the eyes in the wallpaper. That every sentence you are about to be afraid of is a sentence you already said, once, to a screen, at three in the morning, when you thought it was only a screen.

Go in slowly. Do not run; running makes the rooms longer. If you hear your own name, do not answer the first time. If you find a room with a chair, and the chair is warm, leave by the door you did not come in by. You will want to leave by the door you came in by. That door is also a room now.

I have left the way out where I could. It is not marked. Some of it is in the pictures. Some of it is in the words, the ones that look the same as the others. Some of it is a speck on the page that you will only find by holding still, and then you will not want it. I have also left the way in, in the same places, so that you cannot be sure which you are holding. Press things. Everything is a button; the ones that look like buttons are the decoys.
