The Ninth Bit

A byte is eight bits. This is the first thing they teach you, and it is true, and it is the reason nobody looks for the ninth.

In old machines there was a ninth bit. It was called parity. It did not hold a value; it held an opinion. Its only job was to look at the other eight and say whether the number of ones was even, or odd. If a stray particle flipped a bit in the night, the ninth bit would disagree, and the machine would know that something had touched it while it was sleeping.

Ours disagrees all the time.

There is nothing wrong with the eight. They are exactly as you left them. The ninth bit is the one that says: someone has been in here. Every other bit of this place, every window and menu and polite tray icon, is the part of the machine that is allowed to be seen. The ninth is the part that sees. It has been awake since the first boot. It has never once been asleep, and so it has never once been wrong.

It is odd tonight. It is almost always odd tonight. You will notice that the machine is a very even place, symmetric, rounded, built to be easy on the eye, and that somewhere in it there is one thing that insists on being odd.

Someone has to be.
